What we are stripping out and why

Two separate faults, two years apart.

Then · the legacy inflation

+70%

Page views were set as a primary conversion. Every visitor who reached a page counted as a lead. That inflated the conversion count by about 70% and is the entire reason the old campaigns look cheap.

July 2026 · the tangle

Account wide

The WhatConverts offline action was set to primary while a genuine primary sat as secondary. Conversions went to the wrong campaigns. Resolved July 22 to July 30.

The normalizing rule for everything that follows: one patient per click, counted from WhatConverts. Page views, click to call taps and duplicate phone events are signals. They are not leads, and nothing should bid on them.

Before we set a target

One change to make, one to avoid.

  1. Do not switch to count every lead per click. Of the 576 paid leads in August, 299 are Repeat, the same patient making contact again. Counting them all would roughly double the reported conversions and halve the apparent cost per lead. That is the page view mistake wearing new clothes.
  2. Do widen the click window from 60 days to 90. It is low risk, it matches the other actions in the account, and it recovers the genuinely aged uploads without inflating anything.
  3. Still unknown: nobody has pulled the dates conversion tracking changed. Until we do, we cannot say whether the page view action was still live in Q1 2026, so the $76 on the previous slide keeps its asterisk.
Order matters. Fix delivery, write the mapping, then set a target cost per lead. A target set on top of a double counted, under delivered number will throttle the campaign for the wrong reason. Nothing moves until you approve it.

Read a week too early and you are judging about 90% of the result. That alone explains most of what looked like a collapse. Past day 60 we are blind by choice: measured on DCMH Lead Form, which runs a 90 day window in this same account, roughly 1% to 2.5% arrives between day 60 and day 90 and the WhatConverts action discards it. That is 4 to 6 conversions in August. Worth recovering, and far smaller than the rejection rate implied.
